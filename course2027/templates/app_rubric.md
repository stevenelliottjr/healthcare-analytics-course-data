# App rubric: midterm (week 8) and final (week 14)

The same five criteria apply at both points. The final expects more: the midterm column describes "meets expectations" in week 8, and the final column describes it in week 14.

Each criterion is scored 0–4: **4** exceeds · **3** meets · **2** partly meets · **1** attempted · **0** missing.

| Criterion | Midterm (week 8): meets expectations | Final (week 14): meets expectations |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Numbers traced** | You can trace a number on screen, chosen by someone else, back to the source file: file, key, column, value. | Any number on screen can be traced, and your checks (traces, test questions) rerun when the data refreshes. |
| **2. Provenance visible** | The main screen shows each source, publisher and data period. | As midterm, plus the download date or "data as of" and any period mismatch between sources, stated plainly. |
| **3. Exclusions explained** | The app shows how many rows are shown out of how many, and why the rest are excluded. Suppressed values are never shown as zero. | As midterm, plus places that dropped out of joins (for example, territories or Connecticut) are named, and the agent says "I don't know" rather than guessing. |
| **4. Useful to a non-technical reader** | One clear question, one main view, controls instead of code, and plain-language labels. | A reader outside the course can use it without help and make the decision it was built for. Peer review feedback has been acted on. |
| **5. Explanation holds up under questions** | In 5 minutes you explain how you know the numbers are right, and you answer classmates' questions directly. You say what you decided that the AI could not. | You answer unexpected questions with evidence from your work and say plainly what you don't know. Your reflection names specific decisions only a person could make. |

**Total:** 20 points.

**Automatic conversation with the instructor (not a penalty, a check):** any PHI, credentials or workplace data in the app or transcripts, or a suppressed value shown as a number.

## Notes for presenters

- Practice tracing one number live. Expect classmates to pick a different one.
- "I don't know yet, but here's how I'd check" is a good answer.
- Smaller and fully defended beats bigger and partly defended.
