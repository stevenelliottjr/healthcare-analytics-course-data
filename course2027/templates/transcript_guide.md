# AI transcript guide

Submit the AI transcript with each deliverable. Three of your transcripts are graded (15% of the course), chosen **after** you submit, so every transcript counts.

## How to export a transcript

Tools change often, so these are general instructions. Use whatever your tool currently offers.

1. **Share link.** Many chat assistants can create a link to a conversation. If yours can, make sure the link opens when you're signed out (test it in a private browser window) and submit the link.
2. **Export or download.** Some tools can export a conversation as a file (text, Markdown, PDF). Submit the file.
3. **Copy and paste.** If neither works, copy the whole conversation, your messages and the tool's, into a document and save it as a PDF.
4. **Coding agents** (Claude Code or similar, which run on your computer): use the tool's own export or save option if it has one. Otherwise copy the session from your terminal into a text file. Include your project-instructions file if you used one.

**Always:**

- Submit the **whole** conversation for the week's work, including the dead ends. Don't edit it.
- If you used more than one tool or session, submit each one and label them.
- Before you submit, check that the transcript contains **no PHI, no credentials, and nothing from your workplace**. If you find something, don't delete it quietly: tell the instructor.

## What graders look for

The question is: **did you direct and check the tool, or just ask it to finish the work?**

| Strong transcripts show you... | Weak transcripts look like... |
|---|---|
| Stating the goal, the data, and the constraints ("don't convert Not Available to zero; print row counts") | "Do my week 4 assignment" |
| Asking it to explain what it did, in plain language | Accepting code you can't describe |
| Checking its output against the source or a count, and saying so | Never checking, or "looks good!" |
| Catching a mistake and directing the fix | Pasting errors back without reading them, again and again |
| Asking it to challenge you ("what would a skeptic ask?") | Letting it decide what your defense or reflection says |
| Making the decision yourself and saying why | Letting the tool pick sources, exclusions or conclusions |

You won't lose credit for the AI making mistakes. You earn credit for noticing them.

## Writing with AI

You may use AI to help draft written work, including defenses, positions and reflections. The ideas and decisions must be yours. Your live explanation in class, without the AI open, is what verifies that, so draft only what you can explain.
