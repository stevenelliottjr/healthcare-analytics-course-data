# Week 14: Showcase and reflection

**No notebook this week** · [Course home](../README.md) · [Rubric](../templates/app_rubric.md)

## Why this week matters

You started with one chart and one question. You now have a documented data layer, an app and an agent, built on public CMS and CDC data, that you can defend number by number. Tonight you show it to people who will ask hard questions, and you write down the part of the work that was yours alone.

## The idea

The final app is graded on the same standard as everything else: numbers traced, provenance visible, exclusions explained, useful to a non-technical reader, and an explanation that holds up under questions. The reflection asks the course's central question: **what did you decide that the AI couldn't?**

## In class

- **Showcase.** Each student demos their final app (time per student will be announced) and answers questions from the class.
- **Class vote for best app.** Vote for the app you'd most trust to put in front of a real decision-maker. Voting details will be announced in class.
- Closing discussion: what you'd do differently, and what you'd take to work.

## Your one required deliverable

1. Your **final app**, shared as a link, demoed in class. Graded with the [app rubric](../templates/app_rubric.md) (final column).
2. **One page of reflection: what did you decide that the AI couldn't?** Give two or three specific decisions from your project (a source you rejected, an exclusion you made, an answer you refused to trust) and why each one needed a person.

## Steps

1. Act on your peer review: fix the one or two things that matter most. Don't add features.
2. Rerun every check one last time: row counts, traces, compliance checklist, test questions.
3. Rehearse your demo with one number traced live.
4. Write your reflection. AI may help you draft it, but the decisions must be yours, and you should be ready to talk about any of them in class. A useful prompt for testing it:
   > Here is my reflection on what I decided that the AI couldn't: [paste]. For each decision, ask me one question that tests whether it really needed a human. Don't rewrite anything.
5. Open your app link in a private browser window one last time.

## The check to run before you submit

Does every decision in your reflection point to **something visible in your work**: a line in your join log, data card, sign-off or test set?
