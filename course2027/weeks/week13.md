# Week 13: Peer review

**No notebook this week:** [peer review template](../templates/peer_review.md) · [Course home](../README.md)

## Why this week matters

In a hospital analytics team, no number reaches the board without a second person looking at it. A colleague who didn't build the join is the one who notices that Connecticut is missing, or that a rate from 2021–2024 sits next to one from 2023–2025. This week you are that colleague for a classmate, and they are that colleague for you.

## The idea

Review the **work**, not the person. Use the same standard you've used all term: can each number be traced, is provenance visible, are exclusions explained, is it compliant, and would a non-technical reader understand it? A good review finds one or two things that matter and says exactly where they are.

## In class

- Show and explain: four or five of you show your week 12 change report.
- You'll be paired with a classmate and swap app links, data cards and join logs.
- Breakouts: review in pairs, then talk through your findings face to face.

## Your one required deliverable

A completed [peer review](../templates/peer_review.md) of one classmate's aggregator and app, including **one number you traced yourself** from their app to the source file.

## Steps

1. Open your classmate's app link, data card and join log.
2. Trace one number from their app back to the source file, by yourself.
3. Work through the checklist on the template. For each item, mark pass or needs work, with the evidence.
4. You may use your AI tool to look for things you missed, but the judgments are yours:
   > Here is a classmate's data card and a description of their app: [paste]. Using these criteria (numbers traced, provenance visible, exclusions explained, compliance, useful to a non-technical reader), list questions I should ask them. Don't grade it.
5. Write your top two suggestions, specific and kind.

## The check to run before you submit

Does every "needs work" mark point to a **specific place** (a screen, a row, a line on the card) that your classmate can find?
